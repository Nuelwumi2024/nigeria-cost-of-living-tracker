# Executive brief: Nigerian cost of living, September 2026

**For:** CFO / Head of Reward / Head of Pricing · **Data:** NBS CPI, base 2024 = 100, to Sep 2026

## The headline
Inflation has fallen from 34.8% (Dec 2024) to **14.6%**, but prices are still rising faster than most pay rises, and **food inflation is climbing again (19.6%)** after bottoming at 8.9% in January 2026.

## What it means
1. **Food and eating out are about two-thirds of inflation.** Lower-income households spend the most on food, so they feel it first.
2. **Purchasing power has roughly halved since May 2023.** ₦100,000 then needs about ₦204,000 now.
3. **The ₦70,000 minimum wage buys what about ₦48,000 did in July 2024.** Matching its July 2024 value takes about ₦102,000 today.
4. **Rural households are seeing less inflation than urban ones** (12.5% vs 15.6%).

## One thing to treat with care
The published all-items index drops about 2.8% every January since 2025 (it never fell by more than 0.8% in any January from 1996 to 2024). If that is a methodology feature rather than real price falls, cumulative inflation is understated. The honest range for lost purchasing power since May 2023 is **51% to 55%**.

## Outlook to March 2027
About **12%** (80% range roughly 8%-16%). If the January dip does not repeat, year-on-year inflation will jump to ~18% in January 2027 purely from a base effect.

## Recommended actions
- **Reward/HR:** benchmark pay bands against ₦102k-₦112k, not ₦70k.
- **Pricing:** plan for food-led cost pressure; stress-test at 12-16%.
- **Finance:** budget on both scenarios; do not react to January 2027 y/y until the base effect is understood.
- **Everyone:** quote the CPI vintage and flag the January behaviour.

*Limits: national average basket; minimum-wage dates are assumptions; the forecast backtest predates the 2025 rebasing, so its error bands are probably too narrow.*
